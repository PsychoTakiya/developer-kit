# 3. Senior Signals, Questions to Ask & Negotiating Offers

## What changes between SWE II and Senior

Interviewers calibrate your level from every round, not only behavioral. Aim to show the right-hand column.

| Area | SWE II (solid mid-level) | Senior |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Delivers features well within a team | Owns a system or area; work spans teams |
| Ambiguity | Clarifies requirements with help | Turns vague problems into a plan; defines the requirements |
| Technical decisions | Makes good choices within a design | Writes the design, weighs trade-offs, gets buy-in |
| Quality | Writes tested, reviewed code | Raises the bar: testing strategy, observability, code review standards |
| Operations | Fixes bugs, joins on-call | Leads incident response, drives root-cause fixes |
| People | Helps teammates | Mentors, onboards, unblocks; improves how the team works |
| Communication | Clear status updates | Tailors to the audience; explains trade-offs to non-engineers |

**Honest positioning:** with 3.5 years, many companies will place you at SWE II and some at Senior, depending on their ladder. Apply to both. In interviews, consistently showing the senior column is what moves you up.

## Behavioral signals by round

- **Coding:** clarify before coding, talk through trade-offs, test your own code, handle edge cases without prompting.
- **System design:** drive the conversation, state assumptions, discuss failure modes and operational concerns.
- **Behavioral:** stories with clear personal ownership, measurable impact, and influence on others.
- **Hiring manager:** you understand business impact, you know what you want next, and you'd raise the team's level.

## Questions to ask your interviewers

Always ask 2–3. Good questions are another seniority signal. Pick based on who you're talking to.

**To engineers**

- What does a typical week look like for you? How much time goes to meetings vs focused work?
- How do you decide what to build, and how much say do engineers have?
- What does your deploy process look like? How often do you ship to production?
- Tell me about a recent incident and what changed afterwards.
- What's the biggest technical challenge the team faces in the next year?

**To the hiring manager**

- What would success look like in this role after 6 months? After a year?
- What's the difference between a good and a great engineer on your team?
- How are promotions decided, and what did the last person promoted on the team do?
- Why is this position open?
- How do you support growth: mentoring, conferences, learning time?

**To senior leaders / skip-level**

- What are the team's top priorities this year, and how does this role contribute?
- What's something you'd like the engineering culture to do better?

## Handling tricky questions

| Question | Approach |
|---|---|
| "What's your biggest weakness?" | A real but non-fatal one, plus what you're doing about it. Example: "I used to say yes to too many requests and context-switch a lot. Now I agree priorities with my lead weekly and push back with trade-offs." |
| "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" | Growth that fits the role: "Leading larger technical projects, mentoring, possibly moving toward staff or tech lead." |
| "Why should we hire you?" | Three points: relevant skills, a matching achievement with numbers, and motivation for this specific role. |
| "Do you have other offers?" | Be honest without detail if you prefer: "I'm in final rounds with a couple of companies, but this role is one of my top choices because …" |
| "What are your salary expectations?" (early) | Try to defer: "I'd like to learn more about the role and level first. I'm sure we can find a number that works if it's a fit." If pressed, give a researched **range** with your target near the bottom. |
| "Why so long / short at your company?" | A factual, positive reason; focus on what you learned and why you're moving now. |

## Negotiating an offer

1. **Research the market** before the first call: levels.fyi, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox (India), Blind, and peers. Know the range for your level and city.
2. **Don't accept on the call.** Thank them, express enthusiasm, and ask for the offer in writing with a breakdown.
3. **Know the components:** base salary, bonus (target and how it's actually paid), equity (type, vesting schedule, cliff, refreshers), joining bonus, relocation, notice period buyout, benefits.
4. **Negotiate the total**, and ask: "Is there flexibility on the base / equity / joining bonus?" Competing offers are your strongest lever, but only mention ones you have.
5. **Ask about level.** If they've placed you at SWE II and you believe you showed Senior, ask what drove the decision. Re-levelling changes your pay band and future growth more than a small salary bump.
6. **Get the final offer in writing** before resigning. Check the start date against your notice period.

**Script:**

> "Thank you, I'm really excited about this role and the team. Based on my research and other conversations I'm in, I was expecting a total compensation closer to **[X]**. Is there flexibility to get closer to that, for example on the base or the joining bonus? If we can get there, I'm ready to accept."
