# 2. "Tell Me About Yourself" & Project Deep Dive

## "Tell me about yourself" (60 to 90 seconds)

This answer sets the tone for the whole interview. It's not your life story; it's a **pitch for why you fit this role**. Structure: **present → past → why this role**.

### Template

> I'm a **[title]** at **[company]** with about **3.5 years** of experience building **[web applications / products]** with **[React, TypeScript, Node.js, …]**.
>
> Right now I **[own / work on] [product or area]**, where I **[1–2 highlights with impact: "led the migration to X, which cut Y by Z%"]**.
>
> Before that, I **[earlier role or project, one sentence, showing growth]**.
>
> Across these, what I enjoy most is **[the theme: building performant UIs / owning features end to end / scaling systems / developer experience]**.
>
> I'm now looking for a role where I can **[take on more ownership / work on larger-scale systems / have more product impact]**, which is why **[this company / team]** caught my attention: **[one specific reason: product, scale, tech, mission]**.

### Tips

- Write it, then **cut it by a third**. Say it out loud until it sounds natural, not memorised.
- Mention 1–2 things you'd **like them to ask about**; that steers the conversation to your strengths.
- Tailor the last sentence for every company.
- Don't list every technology. Name the ones relevant to the job description.

## "Why are you leaving?"

Keep it **forward-looking**, honest and short. Never criticise your current employer.

- "I've learned a lot in my current role, especially [X]. I'm looking for [larger scale / more ownership / a product I'm closer to / a stronger engineering culture around Y], and this role offers that."
- "I've grown from [junior work] to [owning features end to end], and I want a role where I can grow into [senior responsibilities: leading projects, mentoring, design]."

## "Why this company?"

Research for 20 minutes: the product (use it), the engineering blog, recent launches, the tech stack from the job description. Give **one specific reason about the product or problem** and **one about the team or engineering**.

## Project deep dive

Almost every loop includes "walk me through a project you worked on", often for 30–45 minutes with a hiring manager or senior engineer. They will go deep. Prepare **one main project** and **one backup** using this template.

### Template

```md
## Project: <name>

### 1. Context (1 minute)
- What is the product, who are the users, and why did this project exist?
- Team size and my role.

### 2. Architecture
- Draw it: client, services, databases, queues, third parties.
- Data flow for the main use case.
- Tech choices and WHY each one (and what else was considered).

### 3. My contributions
- The 2–3 hardest things I personally built or decided.
- Trade-offs I made and what I gave up.

### 4. Results
- Metrics: performance, reliability, adoption, revenue, developer time.
- How it was measured.

### 5. Problems and how I handled them
- A bug or incident, and its root cause.
- A disagreement and how it was resolved.

### 6. Looking back
- What I'd do differently with what I know now.
- What would break at 10× scale, and how I'd fix it.
```

### Follow-up questions to prepare for

| They ask | They're checking |
|---|---|
| "Why did you choose X over Y?" | Did you make decisions or just follow? |
| "How did you test it?" | Quality and engineering discipline |
| "How did you deploy and monitor it?" | Production ownership |
| "What was the hardest bug?" | Debugging depth |
| "What would break at 10× traffic?" | Scaling judgement |
| "What would you change today?" | Self-awareness and growth |
| "How did you work with product/design?" | Collaboration |
| "What was your specific contribution?" | Separating "I" from "we" |

### Draw it

Practise drawing your project's architecture in **under 3 minutes**, on paper or in Excalidraw. In virtual interviews, have a clean diagram ready to share if they allow it.
